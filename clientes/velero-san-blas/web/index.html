Activities

There's more than lying on deck

Snorkeling

Shallow reefs, starfish beds and a wreck you can swim over. Gear is on board and the captain knows where the water is clearest that day.

Fishing

Trolling between islands for mackerel and barracuda. If it bites, it's dinner — cooked on board the same evening.

Guna community visit

San Blas is the autonomous territory of the Guna people. We visit a village with respect, buying molas directly from the families who make them.

Getting here

How to reach San Blas from Panama City

This is the part everyone worries about. It's simpler than it looks, and we help you organize it.

Book your 4x4 transfer

A shared 4x4 picks you up at your hotel in Panama City around 5:00–5:30 AM. The drive to Cartí port takes about 2.5 to 3 hours. We can arrange it for you.

Pay the Guna Yala entrance fee

San Blas is Guna autonomous territory. There is a fee per person at the checkpoint, paid in cash, in US dollars. Bring small bills.

Take the boat from Cartí

At the port, a small boat takes you to where we are anchored. Around 20 to 40 minutes, depending on the anchorage that day.

Come aboard

We'll be waiting with everything ready. Shoes off, bags in the cabin, and the trip starts right there.

Flying is also possible: small planes fly from Albrook (Panama City) to several San Blas airstrips. Ask us and we'll tell you if it's worth it for your dates.

FAQ

Questions people ask before booking

Is there wifi or phone signal?

Signal is patchy and there is no wifi on board. That's part of it. If you need to be reachable, tell us and we'll plan anchorages with better coverage.

Do I need to know how to sail?

No. The captain handles the boat. If you want to help, you're welcome to — many guests end up at the helm.

What if I get seasick?

San Blas is protected by reefs, so the water inside the archipelago is calm. The crossings between islands are short. Bring medication if you're sensitive.

Is there a bathroom and a shower?

Yes, X heads on board with fresh-water showers. Water is limited, so we ask for short showers — the sea is right there anyway.

What should I bring?

Light clothing, swimsuit, reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, cash in small US bills for the Guna fees and molas, and any medication you take. Leave hard suitcases behind — soft bags only, there's no room for shells.
